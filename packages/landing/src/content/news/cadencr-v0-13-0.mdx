**CadencR v0.13.0 gives you more control over how you see and follow your agents.**

The sidebar now adapts to your way of working, from a quiet list of conversations to a detailed view across projects. On macOS, the notch keeps running agents, requests, and new replies within reach. Smaller additions make the rest of the workflow easier: open code beside the agent, return to an earlier prompt, see your recent usage before starting a conversation, and choose how that conversation gets its name.

## A sidebar that works the way you do

Start with one of four presets, then adjust the details you want to see. A live preview uses sample conversations to show working agents, pending requests, unread replies, and errors before you settle on a layout.

| Preset        | What it brings forward                                                                          |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Classic**   | Balanced defaults, with status and useful worktree details.                                     |
| **Focus**     | Titles and quiet status marks; waiting conversations reveal the details they need.              |
| **Supervise** | Models, the latest reply, todo progress, and an In progress block for following several agents. |
| **Detailed**  | Branches, diffs, pull requests, ports, and compact rows with room for longer titles.            |

You can group conversations by project, status, worktree, provider, label, or date; choose their sort order; and save a filtered view to return to later. Typing while a sidebar row has focus starts a conversation filter, and keyboard actions let you move to the next request, pin, rename, label, or archive a conversation.

When you collapse the sidebar, a narrow rail can keep projects or conversations accessible. Display choices follow you to connected devices, and the sidebar remembers its width and expansion state. Applying a preset over your custom display options can be undone.

## Your agents, in the macOS notch

On macOS, a compact island keeps your agents present at the top of the screen. It shows activity while they work, briefly opens when a turn finishes or needs your input, and expands on hover into a list of conversations.

**Always on** controls how present the island stays between notifications. **Always animate** controls whether its compact logo keeps moving; with it off, the closed island keeps a still logo. Manage both in **Settings → Notifications**.

The notch is now the default notification destination on macOS, while notifications you have explicitly turned off stay off. Devices that cannot display it fall back to system notifications.

## Smaller additions for the everyday workflow

### Keep the conversation beside the code

**Auto layout** opens browser pages and file references beside the agent, keeping the conversation visible as you inspect the result. It is enabled by default, remembers pane sizes, and respects your manual layout changes. You can turn it off for a conversation or the workspace, and undo an automatic rearrangement.

### Return to what you asked

The new **message navigator** marks each of your prompts along the conversation's right edge. Hover a mark to preview the message, then click to jump there, or use **Alt+Up / Alt+Down** to move between prompts. Older prompts can be reached without loading every intervening turn.

### See your usage before the first message

A new conversation now shows your activity over the last **30 days** before you send its first prompt. Switch between **Messages** and **Tokens**, and inspect a day for the breakdown by provider and model. Usage Stats also gains message totals and daily averages, with the same daily cell grids and day cards.

### Give conversations names that suit you

The **auto-naming prompt** is now editable in Settings. It starts with the built-in instructions, so you can adjust the style or language of conversation titles and reset it whenever you want.

New conversations also start with the provider and model from your last accepted manual prompt across projects. Conversation overrides still take priority.

The release improves scrolling through long conversations, restores thinking choices consistently when models or providers change, makes provider startup more reliable, and brings worktree setup status into a cleaner conversation header. Checkpoints also capture local edits that a copied Git index could previously miss.
